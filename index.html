Research

My research is about how machines understand human movement: reading it from the sensors people already wear, describing and controlling it with language, and generating it in 3D. Increasingly, I am interested in interaction, in agents that pick up what a person means by a movement and respond at the right moment.

2026arXiv

SetCon: Setup-Conditioned IMU-Language Learning from 3D Human Motion

An IMU-language model for zero-shot activity recognition that estimates each device’s mounting from a few unlabeled windows.

65.3 % mean zero-shot accuracy on 13 real datasets, state of the art ahead of AnyMo (46.7 %) and UniMTS (43.2 %)

Lennart Schiweck, Alaa Saleh, Cristóbal Curio

2026arXiv

MinPair: Measured Minimal Pairs for Fine-Grained Control in Text-to-Motion Generation

Caption pairs from real motion clips that differ in one measured attribute teach a text-to-motion model to follow single-word edits.

60 % of edits followed, against at most 22.5 % for published generators trained on up to 99 times more motion data

Lennart Schiweck, Cristóbal Curio

Characters in VR chat with each other while you look around, and fall silent the moment you speak.
